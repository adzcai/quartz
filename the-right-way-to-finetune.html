the right way to finetune

7 min read

Let’s say you have a pretrained model and now you want to get it to do something: solve math problems, do chain of thought reasoning, learn a new human language. How should you do this?

Let’s assume you can sample rollouts from the policy (i.e. take actions in the environment to gain experience).

just reward function

This is the typical reinforcement learning for language problem. We might either have a verifiable reward or a reward model. Pick a reinforcement learning algorithm off the shelf: Dr. GRPO, RLOO, A-star-PO, SPO, any of the others that I must have missed since these, and tune hyperparameters for profit.

just preferences

If you have a dataset of comparisons, you can train a reward model using rlhf — there are many new approaches such as LiPO for list rankings — and then use it for one of the above RL algorithms.

just a dataset

Here’s the interesting and, in my experience, under-discussed part. How should we make use of expert data (which is off policy)? Recent blog posts have pointed out ^[LoRA without regret, Thinking through how pretraining vs RL learn] that a binary reward only provides one bit of information per rollout while supervised data provides bits (I use for the time horizon of a rollout i.e. number of actions).

Until recently most people would call this a supervised learning problem. Which in a sense is true since we do have “labelled data.” But what exactly is the input-output mapping we are learning?

In standard next token prediction training (i.e. “supervised finetuning”), the input is the context window, the output is the “correct” next token, and we minimize negative log likelihood (i.e. maximum likelihood estimation).

But hold on: Supervised learning / MLE assumes the samples are iid. But the samples described above are not iid! Text generation is actually a sequential decision making problem — specifically, given demonstrations, an imitation learning problem! (This assumes we don’t observe rewards. See dataset and reward function for the case with rewards.)

issues with SFT

What’s wrong with SFT? Recent papers suggest that SFT leads to models that don’t generalize well out-of-distribution and even forget capabilities present in the base model ^[2025ChuEtAlSFTMemorizesRL, 2025ShenfeldEtAlRLsRazorWhy].

language model experiments

A 2024 paper ^[2024WulfmeierEtAlImitatingLanguageScalable] finetuned the T5 and PaLM 2 models on grade school math and summarization using IQ-Learn and achieved slightly better same task performance as MLE but with slightly more “diverse” outputs (measured by BLEU i.e. n-gram overlap).

They minimize the Chi-squared distance, which can intuitively be thought of as the squared probability that the learner rolls out a trajectory that the expert never would. ^[SequenceMatch]

on-policy SFT

For binary reward problems, you can also do “on-policy SFT,” in which we collect rollouts and gradient descend on the negative-log-likelihood on the ones that led to correct answers. This is exactly the original REINFORCE since achieving zero reward is the same as discarding the rollout.

just expert

Then you can fix your policy with DAgger and later variants. But these typically aren’t useful in practice since collecting expert human feedback online is impractical, and if you’re collecting AI feedback then naive knowledge distillation is probably better. Theory suggests that online feedback is especially useful for problems where it is easy to recover from “bad” states. ^[Of moments and matching]

dataset and reward function

This is the standard off policy reinforcement learning problem (with the additional assumption that your data is high-quality). How should you update the policy parameters based on data generated by a different policy ?

Note that PPO and its family of algorithms only keep the corrections and assume i.e. that the proportion of time that the policy visits each state stays the same. This results in a biased estimate (especially after clipping) but reduces the variance enough to make it worth it. There exists the DICE family of methods if you really need unbiased estimates of the correction term.

This doesn’t get you a free lunch though: The importance-weighted estimator will have much higher variance, especially if the on-policy vs old trajectory distributions differ significantly.

The field of offline reinforcement learning focuses on the case when we can’t sample rollouts but the dataset contains observed rewards (unlike in imitation learning).
